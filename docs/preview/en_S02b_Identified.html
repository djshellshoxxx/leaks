Safety tips for each step

These tips explain how people who report are most often found, and what you can do about it. Read the higher-risk parts if any of the higher-risk signs on the Safety Check page apply to you. Opening them is not sent to anyone.

What this site cannot do

No tool can hide you completely. This site cannot hide what your files and words reveal, or what a watched device sees.

If you are at higher risk

On this website version, someone who takes over or legally forces the server while you use it could capture what you type and your passphrase. For the highest risk, use the Candor Source App, downloaded over Tor, ideally on Tails.

Your device

Use your own computer, not one from work. Work devices can record what you type and which sites you open.

If you are at higher risk

Use a computer that has never been used for work or joined to a work account, or start it from Tails on a new USB stick. Phones are harder to keep private because they record location and app use. If you only have a phone, use Tor Browser for Android at Safest and close it when you finish.

Your network

Don't use a work or school network. Whoever runs a network can see that you use Tor, and when.

If you are at higher risk

A bridge in Tor Browser makes Tor harder to spot, but it does not fully hide it. Use a network that is not linked to your name, and don't use the same one every time. On public Wi-Fi, sit away from cameras and other screens, and leave your phone at home so its location history does not place you there.

Your browser

Use Tor Browser set to Safest. A VPN or a "private window" in another browser does not protect you in the same way.

If you are at higher risk

Get Tor Browser only from the Tor Project, over Tor if you can, and check its signature. If a yellow JavaScript warning shows on this page, set the security level to Safest before you go on. When you finish, choose New Identity and close the browser.

Where you are

Make sure no one can see your screen. Think about people behind you, windows and cameras.

If you are at higher risk

Places can be checked against camera footage, card payments and phone location records. Switch off or leave your phone before you set out, not when you arrive. Pay cash, and don't keep going back to the same place. Pick times that fit your normal routine.

Accounts and searches

Don't log in to email, social media or any other account while you use this site.

If you are at higher risk

Logging in links what you do to you. Don't look up the people or the subject of your report from work or personal accounts around the time you report. If you need to look something up, do it in Tor Browser without logging in, and not only right before you send.

Timing

Don't send right after you open, copy or print documents. Systems often record who looked at what, and when.

If you are at higher risk

Investigations often start with a list of who opened a file and then look at who did something unusual soon after. Wait days or weeks if you safely can, and don't send right after a meeting or a news story. On the review step you can choose to deliver your report after a random delay of 1 to 3 days.

Files and copies

Describe or retype what you know when you can. Original files can carry hidden names, dates and device details.

If you are at higher risk

A document may be a unique copy made for only a few people. Small changes in wording, spacing or hidden marks can show whose copy it was. Send only what many people had, or describe it in your own words. Never send something only you could open.

Hidden data in files

Photos and files can hide names, places and the device used. File names are replaced by default.

If you are at higher risk

Cleaning does not remove everything. A camera leaves its own pattern in every photo, and the background, a reflection or a user name on a screen can point to you. Office files can keep tracked changes and old versions. Keep originals out of cloud folders and synced phones.

Printing, screenshots and photos of screens

Don't print documents to send them. Printers keep records, and many add tiny tracking dots to each page.

If you are at higher risk

A printed page, or a scan of it, can show which printer made it, and print servers log who printed what. A US contractor was identified this way in 2017. Screenshots and photos of a work screen can show your name, your windows or the time.

Details that point to you

Write only what is needed. Leave out details that only you, or a few people, would know.

If you are at higher risk

For each fact, ask who else knew it, and when. If fewer than five people knew it, leave it out or make it less exact. Meetings you attended, your role and words said only to you can all point to you.

Your writing style

Write in short, plain sentences. Your usual words, spelling and greetings can be matched to emails you wrote at work.

If you are at higher risk

Computer tools can compare writing across many people. Avoid your favorite phrases, usual mistakes, emojis and sign-offs. Don't copy text from your own work messages.

AI tools and translators

Don't paste your report into AI tools, translators or grammar checkers. They can keep what you type.

If you are at higher risk

Online writing tools often keep text and link it to your account, device or network. Spell-check add-ons may send text too. Type your report here, in Tor Browser. If you write a draft first, do it on a device you control, never a work device.

Sharing who you are

Anonymous is the default. Share who you are only if you choose to. You can still send a report without it.

If you are at higher risk

In Confidential mode your name is sealed, but the custodians named on this site can open it. If you give contact details, use ones that are not linked to work or to your usual accounts.

Who you name

Who you name, or tick here, can hint at where you work. Tick only what you need.

If you are at higher risk

If only a few people work with the person you tick, your choice narrows down who you might be. The triage team sees your ticks. Name only the roles you need kept away from your report.

Your passphrase

Keep your passphrase in your head, or on paper hidden at home. Never in work email, notes apps or cloud storage.

If you are at higher risk

Learn it by heart, then destroy the paper. A password manager is fine only on a device only you use, and only if it does not sync online. Anyone with your passphrase can read your replies and write as you. Never type it into any other site, and the team will never ask for it.

Checking for replies

Check for replies every few days at most. Take the same care each time: own device, Tor Browser, no work network.

If you are at higher risk

The days you visit could be compared with who used Tor on those days. Visit rarely, from different networks, and never right after an event linked to your report. Put everything into one message rather than many.

Keep the conversation here

Reply only here. Don't move to email, phone or chat, even if someone asks you to.

If you are at higher risk

A message asking you to use another channel, or asking for your passphrase, may be fake. Calls, emails and meetings leave records that can be linked to you. If you must meet, get legal advice first.

After you send

Act as you normally would. Don't tell anyone, including coworkers, and don't hint that you know about a report.

If you are at higher risk

Don't search for news of your report from work devices or accounts you log in to. People who show unusual interest can be noticed. If you are questioned, you can ask to speak to a lawyer first. Keep notes about your report off work systems.

If your device is checked

Don't keep notes or drafts about your report on any phone or computer that others can check.

If you are at higher risk

A checked device can show Tor Browser, downloads and recent files. Tails keeps nothing after shutdown unless you set it to. Don't destroy anything you may be legally required to keep. Get legal advice if you are under investigation.

When you finish

When you finish, choose New Identity in Tor Browser, then close it.

If you are at higher risk

Also check your Downloads folder and any notes you made. On a shared computer, make sure no one sees Tor Browser open and that it is not set to start on its own.

If something goes wrong

If something goes wrong, don't retry many times in a row. Come back later, with the same care.

If you are at higher risk

Many attempts in a short time can stand out on a network. Close Tor Browser and try again another day if you need to. Never switch to a work device, a normal browser or a non-Tor link to make it work.
