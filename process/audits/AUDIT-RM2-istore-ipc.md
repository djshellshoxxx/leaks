## Findings

### AUD-RM2-IPC-01 — `CLOSE_MAILBOX` after a queued rotation deletes nothing but reports "Account deleted"
- Severity: **High**
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/mod.rs` `close_mailbox` (`q.pending.retain(|a| !ct_eq(&a.account.lookup_tag, &tag))`, then `sink.delete_account(tag, …)`); `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/istore.rs:244-248` (`NotFound` ⇒ `Ok(())` on the first attempt); `crates/candor-intake-store/src/server/mod.rs:567-572` (`account_of` → `NOT_FOUND`).
- Category: B7.1/B8.4 (fail closed), CWE-672/CWE-754; KEY-077, API-054/055, SW-15.
- Description: account writes are queued in RAM and flushed every `account_flush_interval` (default 15 min, allowed 1–60 min; SEA-21). After `ROTATE_FINISH` the session already holds the **new** keys, but the store only gets the replacement at the next flush. `CLOSE_MAILBOX` (1) seals the MAILBOX_CLOSED signal, (2) drops the queued write whose *new* tag equals the session tag — that is the rotation itself — and (3) asks the store to delete the *new* tag, which it does not have: `NOT_FOUND`, which `IstoreSink::delete_account` treats as "already gone" even on the first attempt. Result: `Sealed` is returned, the session is removed, the web shows "Account deleted", the case receives the MAILBOX_CLOSED signal, yet the account row (old tag, old keys, `prefs_ct`), its mailbox and all replies remain in the store, no `mailbox`/`account` entry is appended to the K31 list, and the old passphrase logs in again.
- Exploit scenario / impact: no attacker needed; any source that rotates its passphrase and closes the mailbox within one flush window (a natural "burn the account" sequence) keeps a live account it believes destroyed. ADV with later access to the passphrase (coercion, device seizure; the source may not bother to destroy a passphrase it believes dead) or to the intake DB after a restore gets a live mailbox and keys; a restore (RL-12) cannot remove it because nothing was listed. Silent degradation of a promised protection → High.
- PoC (scratch `audit-w1a/poc/tests/poc.rs::ipc01_close_mailbox_after_queued_rotation_deletes_nothing`, real `IstoreServer` + `MemoryStore` + K31 signer, real sealer): submit → flush → login → `RotatePassphrase`/`ConfirmPassphrase`/`RotateFinish` (queued) → `CloseMailbox` = `Sealed`; then `lookup_account(old_tag)` = `Some`, `deletion_list_after(0)` = 0 entries, `flush_accounts()` = `Ok(0)` (replacement dropped), and `LoginDerive(old passphrase)` returns the old tag of the live row. Output: `account under old tag present: true, deletion entries: 0`.
- Fix: in `close_mailbox` (and `delete_replies`), before sealing the signal, resolve the account's queued state: either write any queued entry for this account (match `account.lookup_tag == tag` **or** `replaces == tag`, plus `inflight_replaced`) through the sink synchronously under `flush_lock`, then delete by the tag the store holds; or refuse with `BAD_STATE`/`BUSY` while such an entry is queued (user retries after the flush). In `IstoreSink::delete_account`, treat `NOT_FOUND` as success **only on the retry after a transport failure** (track whether a first attempt was sent), never on a first attempt. Regression test: the PoC (assert the old tag is gone and `mailbox`+`account` entries exist after rotate-then-close within one window), plus the two-session variant of IPC-02.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-02 — A store-refused `ACCOUNT_UPSERT` is requeued forever and wedges every later rotation
- Severity: **High**
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/mod.rs` `write_batch` (`if upsert_account(a).is_err() { requeue([a] ++ rest) }`), `shuffle_batch` (creates first, then replacements in queue order), `rotate_dummy`; `istore.rs::retrying` (`Attempt::Refused` → `SinkError`, indistinguishable from transport failure to the caller).
- Category: B2.6 (bounded queues / poison), B8.4; CWE-400/CWE-703.
- Description: `IstoreSink::upsert_account` returns the same `SinkError` for a transport failure (retry makes sense) and a store refusal (`NOT_FOUND` for a replacement whose old tag is gone, `INVALID`). `write_batch` puts the failing op back at the head of the queue, so a permanently refused replacement fails every flush; because replacements are written after the creates and in order, every replacement queued behind it is never written until the sealer restarts (and the RAM queue is lost). Consequences for every source that rotates in the meantime: the KEY_ROTATION envelope already went to the case (which encrypts future replies to the new key), the old passphrase is blocked at login (SEA-28(a) random locator while a replacement is pending), the new tag is unknown to the store → locked out; after a restart the old passphrase works again but replies wrapped to the new key are unreadable (`OPEN_REPLY` skips them). The failing dummy rotation also holds the dummy chaff account mix in a stuck state.
- Triggers: (a) one source with two sessions of the same account (allowed): S1 rotates (queued, `replaces = T_old`), S2 closes the mailbox (deletes `T_old` in the store; the retain in `close_mailbox` does not match `replaces`) → the queued replacement is `NOT_FOUND` forever. (b) No attacker: `rotate_dummy` picks a dummy tag whose row no longer exists — after an intake restore from a backup predating the dummy's create (14-day BS-INTAKE bound), after the 365-day purge, or after an IPC-01-style race; with `dummy_rotation_permille` per chaff event this is likely within days of any restore.
- PoC (`poc.rs::ipc02_refused_replacement_wedges_every_later_rotation`): account B and attacker account A created and flushed; A logs in twice; `RotateFinish` in session 1; `CloseMailbox` in session 2 (`Sealed`, A's old row deleted); B logs in and rotates; three consecutive `flush_accounts()` return `Err(SinkError)`; B's new tag is absent from the store, B's old row is still there, and `LoginDerive(B's passphrase)` yields a random locator (B is locked out).
- Fix: make a refusal final for that operation only: `IstoreSink::upsert_account` must distinguish `Attempt::Refused` (drop the op, continue the batch, count in health) from transport failure (requeue, stop); `write_batch` should continue after a dropped op. `close_mailbox` must also purge queued entries with `replaces == tag` (or apply them first, IPC-01). `rotate_dummy` should remove a refused dummy from `dummies`. Regression tests: the PoC; a unit test that a batch `[create, bad-replace, replace]` writes both the create and the good replacement.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-03 — Two concurrent `CLOSE_MAILBOX` requests on one session seal two MAILBOX_CLOSED signals
- Severity: Low
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/mod.rs` `close_mailbox`: the guard is moved into `signal_blocking`, then `self.locked(&sess)` is re-acquired and `g.closing = Some(offset)` set; a second request that takes the lock in between sees `closing == None`.
- Category: B7.1 (cancellation/interleaving), CWE-362.
- Description/PoC (`poc.rs::ipc03_concurrent_close_mailbox_seals_two_signals`): `tokio::join!` of two `CloseMailbox` on the same session: both answer `Sealed`, `pending_count` grows by 2 (two signal envelopes to the case; double-submit from the web is enough). Harmless for anonymity (signal shape is a follow-up) but violates "exactly one signal" and the retry-safety claim; also if the second request's `locked()` fails (session expired between) the signal is sealed without `closing` recorded.
- Fix: return the `OwnedMutexGuard` from `signal_blocking` (as `rotate_blocking` does) and set `closing` before the guard is released; the web should also serialise `/end` per session.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-04 — The `account` deletion entry hashes the current `lookup_tag`; a restore from a backup that predates a rotation resurrects a deleted account
- Severity: **Medium** (spec-level; lead decision)
- Location: `crates/candor-intake-store/src/memory.rs:257-278`, `pg.rs:1296-1327` (`account_del_hash(&tenant, &tag)` with the row's current tag); restore matching `memory.rs:931-937`, `pg.rs:996-1016` (match restored rows by their *backup* tag). Spec 04 §18.6 (subject = `lookup_tag`), KEY-077, ADR-046(7).
- Description: a rotation replaces the tag in place; BS-INTAKE backups carry accounts with the tag of the backup day. `CLOSE_MAILBOX` after a rotation lists `H(T_new)`; a restore of an older backup holds `T_old`, so RL-12 deletes nothing and the account shell (old keys, `prefs_ct`) is live again under the old passphrase, with the store serving. Replies are not in backups, and listed `mailbox` entries would drop re-pushed replies once RL-05 can resolve mailboxes (IPC-09), so the impact is an authenticated empty mailbox that the source believes destroyed, plus any reply the relay pushes to it that is not mailbox-listed. Precondition: rotation after the last backup, deletion later, then a DR restore within the 14-day bound.
- PoC (`poc2.rs::ipc04_restore_resurrects_account_deleted_after_rotation`, `MemoryStore`): create (tag 20) → `export_backup` → `update_account` (tag 21) → `delete_account` → new store `restore_backup` + `apply_pushed_deletion_list` (verified Z-CORE head) = `Ok`, `serving_allowed` = true, `lookup_account([20;32])` = `Some`.
- Fix options: (a) hash the intake-local `account_id` (random 128-bit, stable across rotations, present in backups) as the `account` subject (spec amendment to 04 §18.6; Z-CORE needs only an opaque hash); (b) append an additional `account` entry for the old tag at `update_account` time (reveals rotation days in the list; not recommended); (c) rely on `mailbox` entries once a mailbox→account table exists (IPC-09). Recommend (a). Regression test: the PoC.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-05 — Shared connection cap and thread-per-connection before the peer check
- Severity: Low
- Location: `crates/candor-intake-store/src/server/mod.rs` `serve` (spawns a thread per accepted socket), `serve_connection` (peercred, then cap; over-cap waits up to `io_timeout` for a datagram), `ServerConfig::max_connections` (one cap for all roles).
- Description: an unknown uid still costs a thread spawn; an over-cap peer holds a thread for ≤ 5 s; the 128-connection cap is shared, so a misbehaving or compromised web tier (the Tor-facing tier) holding 128 idle connections (idle timeout 300 s) makes every sealer `COMMIT_GROUP`/`ACCOUNT_UPSERT`/`DELETE` answer `BUSY` → every seal fails `INTERNAL`, chaff stops. A compromised web is an outage anyway, so Low; ADR-052(4) intends per-service robustness.
- Fix: per-role caps (web ≤ its pool size plus margin, sealer reserved), check cap and peercred in the accept loop before spawning, a shorter idle timeout for the web role.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-06 — Signal envelopes are distinguishable at rest in the intake store
- Severity: Low (metadata; lead decision)
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/rand.rs` (`release_offset` 1..3, `mailbox_closed_offset` 3..21), `signal_job` (`epoch_index` = release epoch), chaff `delay` drawn from the real-submission distribution (`mod.rs:1856-1880`), store `MAX_RELEASE_OFFSET_DAYS` raised to 21.
- Description: only MAILBOX_CLOSED signals carry `release_offset_days` 4..21 and (whenever the release day crosses an epoch) an `epoch_index` ahead of `received_date`; real and chaff groups use 0..3 and the current epoch. A seized intake DB (02 §6 ADV with host access) therefore reads "a mailbox was closed on day D on channel C" per envelope row; combined with the deletion list's `account` entry of day D this links the channel to the deleted account. 08 SW-15/API-055 only constrain what the case learns, and the deletion list reveals the day by design, so the new information is the channel.
- Fix: have chaff draw signal-shaped groups (kinds 2/3 shape, offsets 1..3 and 3..21, release epoch) at a configured share so the shape is not unique; or spec acceptance by the lead.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-07 — `MAILBOX_READ` reloads the whole mailbox per reply (read amplification)
- Severity: Low
- Location: `server/mod.rs:401-410` (`MailboxRead` → `mailbox_list` then `find`), `client/mod.rs:256-282` (`IstoreClient::mailbox` = LIST + n READs).
- Description: one web mailbox view costs (n+1) full-mailbox loads with every `reply_ct` (up to 32 × 69,996 B): ≈ 72 MB of database reads for a full mailbox per page view, source-triggerable by refreshing. Not unbounded, but a cheap amplifier against the PG store.
- Fix: a `mailbox_read(account, reply_ref)` store operation (single-row query), or return the ciphertexts in several datagrams after one list.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-08 — Replay answers differ and duplicate `mailbox` entries on retry
- Severity: Info
- `Delete::Account` retried after a crash between the `mailbox` and `account` transactions appends a second `mailbox` entry (harmless duplicate); `Delete::Replies` replay returns `Deleted(0)` after a first `Deleted(n)`; the sealer forwards the count to the web (display only). Document; no change required.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-09 — `ACCOUNT_UPSERT.mailbox_ids` not persisted (builder's open spec gap): judgement
- Severity: Info (recommendation), with Medium consequence tracked in IPC-04
- Intake-side deletion is complete without it: `DELETE Account` removes the row and cascades the replies (memory `remove_account`; PG `ON DELETE CASCADE` on `reply.source_account_id`), the `mailbox` entries are appended from the ids the sealer holds in `prefs` (complete for v1: one report), and `Delete::Replies` lists the stored object hashes. What is missing: RL-05 can neither route a pushed reply to an account nor drop it for a listed mailbox (both need mailbox→account), and a restore cannot apply `mailbox` entries to accounts (IPC-04). Because the mapping lives only inside the source-encrypted `prefs_ct`, it cannot be backfilled later; the data is already on the wire. Recommend adding `candor.mailbox (mailbox_id PK, account_id FK ON DELETE CASCADE)` now, filled by `ACCOUNT_UPSERT` (replace on rotation) and purged with the account, rather than deferring to RM-3.

## New findings (round 2)

### AUD-RM2-IPC-10 — A store `UNAVAILABLE`/`BUSY` answer is classified `Refused`: a transient outage dead-letters real account creates and rotations
- Severity: **High** (regression introduced by the IPC-02 fix)
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/istore.rs` `upsert_account` (`Err(ClientError::Store(NotFound)) => Stale`, `Err(ClientError::Store(_)) => Refused`; only `ClientError::Transport` is `Unavailable`); `mod.rs` `write_batch` (`Refused` → `MAX_UPSERT_REFUSALS` = 3 then `dead_letter`).
- Description: the store answers `UNAVAILABLE` (code 5) while restore-pending (every process start of the PG store until RL-12 confirms the head, up to one control cycle), on backend errors and on the op deadline, and `BUSY` (6) on capacity. All of these are known transient conditions with nothing applied, yet the sink reports them as a final refusal. After three flushes (45 min at the default interval, 3 min at the minimum) every queued write — real account creates of new sources, real rotations, dummy writes — is dropped. The source was told "received" and holds a passphrase that will never log in; a rotating source is locked out (old tag blocked by SEA-28(a), new tag never written) and the case already uses the new key. Nothing distinguishes this from IPC-02's wedge for the affected sources, except that it is now silent and permanent per write (a health event only says `QUEUE_BACKLOG`).
- PoC (`poc.rs::r2_new_unavailable_store_dead_letters_real_accounts`, real sealer + `IstoreServer` + `MemoryStore`): submit → `mark_restore_pending()` → three `flush_accounts()` (`Ok(0)`, `Ok(0)`, `Ok(0)` with `dead_letters = 1` after the third) → restore cleared → `flush_accounts() = Ok(0)` → `LoginDerive` of the passphrase: no account in the store.
- Fix: map `ErrorCode::Unavailable | Busy | Internal` (and any undecodable reply) to `UpsertError::Unavailable` (requeue, keep order); treat only `Invalid`/`Forbidden`/`BadFrame` as `Refused` and `NotFound`-on-replacement as `Stale`. Regression test: the PoC (expect the account to exist after the outage and `dead_letters = 0`); a unit test of the mapping table.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-11 — `flush_source` drops a refused write without a dead letter; with IPC-10 a close or reply deletion during an outage silently loses the source's queued account
- Severity: **High** (same root cause as IPC-10; independent fix needed in `flush_source`)
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/mod.rs` `flush_source`: `Ok(()) | Err(Stale) | Err(Refused) => { collect tags }` — the entry is removed from the queue and neither requeued, dead-lettered nor counted.
- Description: `DELETE_REPLIES` and `CLOSE_MAILBOX` first take the source's queued writes out of the queue and write them synchronously. A `Refused` (today: any `UNAVAILABLE`/`BUSY`) result is treated as "written": the create or rotation vanishes without trace, the operation then fails `INTERNAL`, and the source retries against an account the store will never hold. A source that submits and, in the same visit, deletes a reply or closes the mailbox while the store is restore-pending loses its account for good; the health counter stays at 0.
- PoC (`poc.rs::r2_new_delete_replies_during_outage_loses_queued_create`): submit (create queued) → `mark_restore_pending()` → `DeleteReplies { replies: [] }` = `INTERNAL` → restore cleared → `flush_accounts() = Ok(0)` → login: no account; `dead_letters = 0`.
- Fix: in `flush_source`, `Unavailable` (after IPC-10's mapping) requeues and fails (as now); `Refused` should go through `dead_letter` (counted, health event) and `Stale` likewise; neither should be silently discarded. Regression test: the PoC; assert the create is written once the store serves again.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-12 — Signal-shaped chaff is only partly signal-shaped (IPC-06 residual)
- Severity: Low (open; supersedes IPC-06)
- Location: `mod.rs` `chaff()` (`signal_shaped` drawn independently of `followup`; bundle length from `buckets.bundle`); `tests/signal.rs::signal_and_chaff_envelopes_share_their_structure` pins `followup_share_permille: 1000` and does not compare the object type or the bundle length.
- Description: a real signal is always a SOURCE_MESSAGE with an empty (256 KiB) bundle. With the production defaults (follow-up share 300, empty-bundle weight 600/1000) only 18 % of the "signal-shaped" chaff has that shape; the other 82 % (SUBMISSION header, or a non-empty bundle, with offset 4..21 or a future epoch) is identifiable as chaff at rest and gives no cover. Real signals stay hidden among the 18 %, so this is thinner cover, not a new distinguisher of real signals.
- Fix: when `signal_shaped`, force `followup = true` and the empty bundle bucket; extend the structure test to object type and bundle length under production shares.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-13 — No upgrade path for migration 0002: a cluster initialised before it can never be opened by the new build
- Severity: **Medium** (operability; fail-closed; lead decision on the migration process, BE-050)
- Location: `crates/candor-intake-store/src/pg.rs` `schema_hash()` (over all `MIGRATIONS`), `open` (`SQL_META_HASH` must equal `schema_hash()`), `migrate` (writes the ledger only); `migrations/0001_intake_schema.sql:132-166` (`intake_meta_monotonic` raises on any change of `schema_hash`).
- Description: `intake_meta.schema_hash` is written once at `init` with the hash of the migrations compiled into that build. Applying 0002 with `candorctl migrate` records 0002 in the ledger but cannot update `intake_meta.schema_hash` (the guard trigger forbids it for every role, including the migrator), so the new build's `open` fails with `Integrity("schema hash mismatch")` forever; the old build refuses too (`applied.len() != MIGRATIONS.len()`). The only way out is a superuser disabling the trigger, which defeats the immutability guard. The PG suite did not see this because every test migrates a fresh database with both migrations.
- PoC (`poc3.rs::r2_upgrade_path_schema_hash`, through `pg-test.sh`): fresh DB + `migrate` + `init`; the row's hash replaced by the hash a `[0001]`-only build records (trigger disabled by the superuser for the simulation only; with the trigger enabled the `UPDATE` is refused with `P0001 "intake_meta identity is immutable"`); `migrate` again = no-op; `PgIntakeStore::open` = `Err(Integrity("schema hash mismatch"))`.
- Fix: define the schema hash as the hash of the *applied ledger* (or let each migration carry the new expected hash and have `migrate` update `intake_meta.schema_hash` in the same transaction, with the trigger allowing exactly that transition as the migrator role); add an upgrade test (init under `MIGRATIONS[..n]`, apply `n+1`, open). No deployment exists yet, so the impact today is the process, not data.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-14 — Per-source lock table grows without bound
- Severity: Low
- Location: `mod.rs` `source_lock` (inserts), `delete_replies` (never removes), `close_mailbox` (removes only after a confirmed deletion).
- Description: every `DELETE_REPLIES` and every failed close leaves a `(mailbox_id → Arc<Mutex<SourceState>>)` entry for the life of the process (~100 B each). A source can create accounts and call `DELETE_REPLIES` with an empty list for each; growth is bounded only by the account count. `recently_deleted` is bounded (4,096).
- Fix: remove the entry when the lock is released with a default `SourceState`, or bound the table (LRU) and keep `closing`/`delete_sent` only while set.

### Info (no change required)
- `delete_replies` in a second session after another session's rotation was flushed by `flush_source` requests the deletion by the stale session tag → `INTERNAL`; that session's keys are dead anyway (SEA-28(a)). Using the tags returned by `flush_source` would make it succeed.
- A session of a deleted source whose `recently_deleted` record was evicted (after 4,096 later deletions, within its 2 h lifetime) could seal a second MAILBOX_CLOSED signal; implausible, but a bounded-by-time record would close it.
- `SourceState::delete_sent` is also set on a store *refusal* (known outcome, nothing applied), slightly wider than ADR-057(3)'s "unknown outcome"; harmless because the account cannot vanish in between except through another close, which `recently_deleted` covers.

## New finding (round 3)

### AUD-RM2-IPC-15 — The SIGTERM flush honours the backoff: a restart during or after an outage loses every queued account write
- Severity: **High** (same impact as IPC-10; precondition: an unavailable-store episode followed by a sealer stop/restart within the backoff window, which is exactly the incident-handling sequence)
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/mod.rs` `flush_accounts` (`if q.skip_flushes > 0 { … return Ok(0) }`), called unchanged by `shutdown_flush` (SEA-28(c)) and `spawn_sigterm_flush`.
- Description: after one `Unavailable` flush the queue skips the next 1–4 scheduled flushes. `shutdown_flush` enqueues the 1–4 dummy creates and calls `flush_accounts`, which returns `Ok(0)` without writing while `skip_flushes > 0`; the integrator then exits and the RAM queue — real account creates and rotations — is gone. The sources were told "received" and hold a passphrase that never logs in; a rotation's KEY_ROTATION already reached the case. The store being back up at shutdown time does not help.
- PoC (`poc.rs::r3_variant_shutdown_flush_under_backoff`): submit → `mark_restore_pending()` → `flush_accounts()` = `Err` (backoff 1) → restore cleared → `shutdown_flush()` = `Ok(0)` → the account is absent from the store.
- Fix: `shutdown_flush` (and any explicit/operator flush) must clear `skip_flushes`/`backoff` and attempt the write; if the store is still unavailable at exit the queue is lost — document that residual (a durable spool is out of scope) and have the integrator delay exit for a bounded retry. Regression test: the PoC (expect `Ok(≥1)` and the account present).

### AUD-RM2-IPC-16 — No drain around the shutdown pass: a write accepted while the pass runs is reported lost but never attempted; one accepted after the final count is lost silently
- Severity: **Medium**
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/src/server/mod.rs` `shutdown_flush` (2411–2442: one `flush_locked(true)` pass, then `left = pending.len()` read outside `flush_lock`, 2431), `spawn_sigterm_flush` (2448–2463: no "stopping" state), `queue_has_room` (2802–2804: admits writes regardless of shutdown) and the enqueue sites (2254–2260, 3070–3093, 3265–3275); `README.md:59–66` (`serve` keeps accepting until the flush task completes). Commit `f6d47ccd`.
- Category: B7.1 (interleaving at shutdown), B8.4 and 07 §13 (fail closed: a stopping sealer must refuse, not accept and drop), CWE-754 / CWE-404.
- Description: the pass takes the queue once (`core::mem::take(&mut q.pending)`, 2300) and writes it while the sealer still serves. A `SEAL_FINISH` or `ROTATE_FINISH` that completes meanwhile commits its envelope (durable) and enqueues the account write — the source is told "received" — and the pass never sees it. After the pass `left ≥ 1`, so `Err(ShutdownLoss(left))` and one backlog event are produced, but no attempt is made although the store is up, and the integrator exits (README: `_ = term => Ok(())`). A write enqueued after `left` was read is not even counted: `Ok(n)` is returned and the process exits with it in RAM. The SPEC-NOTES residual ("writes still queued when the store is **down** at shutdown are lost at exit") does not cover this case: the store is up the whole time.
- PoC (ran; `audit_r4_poc.rs::p1_create_accepted_during_shutdown_pass_is_never_attempted`, gated sink that blocks the first `upsert_account`): create A queued → `shutdown_flush` on a thread blocks inside the sink with the batch taken → a second session's `SEAL_FINISH` = `Sealed`, `queued_accounts() = 1` → gate opened → `shutdown_flush` = `Err(ShutdownLoss(1))`, `queued_accounts() = 1` (B never attempted), the sink holds A plus 1–4 dummies. The after-count variant is a race: not reproduced, reasoning only (same root cause).
- Exploit scenario: no adversary; every `systemctl stop`/`restart` (SIGTERM) while Tier W traffic flows. The window is the pass duration: sub-second with an empty queue and a healthy store, seconds to minutes after an outage (up to 4,100 sequential store round trips). Impact as IPC-15 for the affected source: told "received", the passphrase never logs in; for a rotation the KEY_ROTATION already reached the case, the old passphrase is blocked until the restart (SEA-28(a)) and then works again with replies to the new key unreadable. Medium rather than High: the loss is reported (except the post-count race), the window is bounded by the pass, and the source notice (SEA-28(d)) already warns about restarts.
- Fix recommendation: (1) a `stopping` flag set by `spawn_sigterm_flush` on `term.recv()` and by `shutdown_flush` at entry, consulted by `queue_has_room` — the check precedes the envelope commit at every enqueue site, so `SEAL_FINISH`/`ROTATE_FINISH` fail closed with `BUSY` and chaff is skipped, nothing half-done; (2) after the pass, re-check `pending` under `flush_lock` and run a bounded number (e.g. 2) of further passes while the store keeps answering, so writes that slipped in before the flag was visible are attempted; (3) read `left` under `flush_lock` after the last pass; (4) README/integrator: stop accepting (drop `serve` or close the listener) before awaiting the flush and exit non-zero on `ShutdownLoss`. Regression tests: P1 adapted (the late `SEAL_FINISH` gets `BUSY`, or B is written and `Ok(n)` returned), and a test that `queue_has_room` is false once stopping.
- Spec / requirement reference: 07 §13 ("Sealer down or killed … never weaker protection"), BE-033, SEA-28(c)/(d), ADR-057(4), SPEC-NOTES "Fixes for AUD-RM2-IPC round 3" (residual wording to be widened by the owners).
- Status: Open.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-17 — Integrator contract gaps: the documented pattern discards `ShutdownLoss`, skips the flush when `serve` returns, has no stop-time bound, and the retry hooks do not fit the SPEC-NOTES advice
- Severity: Low
- Location: `crates/candor-sealer/README.md:59–66`; `deploy/intake/systemd/candor-sealer.service` (no `TimeoutStopSec`; `Restart=on-failure`); `mod.rs` `write_batch` (2343–2344: a failed shutdown attempt re-arms the backoff), `shutdown_flush` (2413–2422: 1–4 new dummies per call). No sealer binary exists yet; the README block is the contract the integrator will copy.
- Category: B7.1, B10.1/B10.2 (unit settings), documentation vs SPEC-NOTES.
- Description: (a) `_ = term => Ok(())` maps `Err(ShutdownLoss(_))` and a failed join to a clean exit, contradicting SPEC-NOTES ("must treat `ShutdownLoss` as a failed flush, not a clean stop"); only the health event remains. (b) If `serve` returns first (listener failure, hardening refusal) the process exits with no flush and no report. (c) The pass is unbounded in time (G9) and the unit sets no `TimeoutStopSec`: systemd's default 90 s kills a long pass (4,096 writes after an outage at ordinary PG commit latency is in that range) and the remainder is lost without the `ShutdownLoss` report. (d) The SPEC-NOTES advice "the integrator may delay exit for a bounded retry" has no fitting API: a retry through `flush_accounts` is skipped once by the re-armed backoff (PoC P5: `Ok(0)`, then `Ok(lost)`), and a retry through `shutdown_flush` adds 1–4 dummies per call.
- PoC: P5 (ran) for (d); (a)–(c) by reading — not reproduced, reasoning only.
- Exploit scenario: none adversarial; operational. (a)/(b) hide a lost queue from the exit status and the restart policy; (c) turns a reported loss into a silent one.
- Fix recommendation: README pattern: stop accepting first, `let r = term.await`, exit non-zero on `Err`, and run `shutdown_flush` on the `serve`-error path too; set `TimeoutStopSec` (≥ the worst pass the deployment accepts, e.g. 300 s like the store unit) and bound the pass (deadline, or a per-pass write cap with the rest reported); make the shutdown attempt not re-arm the backoff, or offer a retry entry point that adds no dummies. Record it in SPEC-NOTES.
- Spec / requirement reference: SEA-28(c), SPEC-NOTES round-3 fix text, 17/18 unit hardening, B10.1.
- Status: Open.

### The lead's questions

1. **Is IPC-16 closed, chaff rotation path and post-count race included?** Closed for every write the sealer admits after the stop: `queue_has_room` is `!stopping && len < MAX` (2971–2974) and is consulted before `commit_group` at all three enqueue sites — initial `SEAL_FINISH` (3252), `ROTATE_FINISH` (3447) and now every chaff shape (2308; a follow-up-shaped event rotates a dummy, which is a queued write too), so a stopping sealer answers `BUSY` with nothing committed (shipped tests `stopping_sealer_refuses_new_writes_and_writes_what_it_took`, `stopping_sealer_refuses_chaff`; my Q3 after a real SIGTERM: `BUSY`, commit counter unchanged). Writes enqueued after the final count are counted, reported and written by the retry (`closed` branch 2982–2990; my Q2: `late_arrivals() = 1`, one backlog event carrying 1, second `shutdown_flush` = `Ok(1)` with no new dummies). **What remains (Low, IPC-19):** (i) a write whose `commit_group` started before SIGTERM and completes after the *last* final count (the retry's) is reported through the health sink and `late_arrivals()` but lost at exit — the lead's documented residual, bounded by one commit's latency; (ii) more than two staggered such arrivals exhaust `SHUTDOWN_PASSES` and are returned as `Err(ShutdownLoss(left))` with the store up, and the SIGTERM task does not retry an `Err` (2593–2598); (iii) in the `closed` branch `arrivals` is incremented, the lock is released for the health call and the write is pushed afterwards (2981–2995), so a retry pass that starts in that gap sees the arrival but not the write. 2. **Can the sticky `stopping` wedge anything that must keep working?** No. `stopping` is read only by `queue_has_room`; `LOGIN_DERIVE` derives locally and consults the queue read-only (`replaced_pending`), `DELETE_REPLIES`/`CLOSE_MAILBOX` go through `flush_source` (`flush_lock` → `accounts`, no room check; they wait for a running pass and then proceed), follow-up seals queue no account write, uploads (`PART_BEGIN`/`PART_CHUNK`) are unaffected; my Q4: a `DELETE_REPLIES` issued while the pass is blocked inside the store waits ≥ 300 ms, completes `Deleted` once the gate opens, then `LOGIN_DERIVE` → `Locator` and `CLOSE_MAILBOX` → `Sealed` on the stopped sealer. The chaff scheduler ignores event errors and sleeps until the next draw (2690–2705: `let _ = self.chaff_event(c).await;` then `sleep_until(wake)`), so the `BUSY` per event after the stop neither spins nor logs. `stopping` is never cleared: a sealer that ran `shutdown_flush` refuses initial seals, rotations and chaff for good, which is the intended end state (the process exits). The user-visible effect equals a restart (07 §13 "please start again") and nothing is committed, so fail-closed holds.

### AUD-RM2-IPC-19 — Remaining unattempted-write windows after the drain: in-flight commits are not awaited before the final count
- Severity: Low
- Location: `mod.rs` `shutdown_flush` (2496–2520: passes and count keyed on `arrivals`, no view of commits in flight), `spawn_sigterm_flush` (2587–2598: one retry after `Ok` only, none after `Err`), `enqueue_account` `closed` branch (2981–2995: `arrivals` incremented before the lock is released for the health call and the write pushed after). Commit `52eb19a2`.
- Category: B7.1 (interleaving at shutdown), CWE-362.
- Description: the drain keys on writes that have *arrived*; a seal admitted before `stopping` is invisible until its `commit_group` returns, which can be after the retry's final count (window: the latency of one store commit plus hand-over, seconds for a large bundle). Such a write is counted (`late_arrivals`), reported (backlog event) and lost at exit — the residual the lead documents in SPEC-NOTES; two further variants are not documented: three or more staggered such arrivals exhaust `SHUTDOWN_PASSES` and are returned as `Err(ShutdownLoss(left))` with the store up, which the SIGTERM task does not retry; and a retry pass starting between the `arrivals` increment and the push in the `closed` branch sees the arrival but not the write. None is silent.
- PoC: the mechanism of a post-count arrival is reproduced (Q2: counted, reported, written by the retry); the loss itself needs the exit to win a race with a commit — not reproduced, reasoning only.
- Exploit scenario: none adversarial; a restart under load with a large bundle in flight. Impact: one source told "received" whose passphrase never logs in, with the loss recorded in the health log and the exit status.
- Fix recommendation: count seals in flight (increment at the room check, decrement after the enqueue or on any failure) and have `shutdown_flush` wait, bounded by the commit deadlines, for zero in-flight seals before the final count; retry an `Err` produced only by exhausted passes; push before releasing the lock in the `closed` branch (or increment `arrivals` after the push). Regression tests: Q1/Q2 plus a seal held inside the commit across the final count.
- Spec / requirement reference: SEA-28(c)/(d), 07 §13, SPEC-NOTES round-4 residual.
- Status: Open.
